The Judgement Deck · Facilitation

Run it as a sixty-minute session.

For a team meeting or a workshop block. Six or more people, one printed deck per table of three or four. The deck does the teaching; you keep the time.

What you need on the table

One printed deck per table (cut before the session) · a pen per person · dot stickers, two per person · a visible timer · one sheet per table for the commitments.

TimeMove
0:00Deal and sort. Each table spreads the deck face up and sorts it into three piles: we already do this, we should do this, nobody does this. No debating yet; sorting only. The piles are the room's honest map.
0:10Place the week's real work. Each person names one piece of AI-assisted work from their week. The table places it on the trust tool's corners together — A, B, C or D — and says what check the corner demands. Disagreement here is the exercise, not a problem.
0:20Read the moves. Tables take turns reading their "we should do this" pile aloud, one line each — the line at the bottom of the card only. Fast, no discussion. This is the room hearing its own to-do list.
0:30Dot-vote the five. Two dots each, on the cards the team most needs in the next month. Dots go on cards, not on people. The top five cards come off the deck and stay on the table.
0:40Name the owners. For each of the five: one name, one change to Monday, one way the team will see it worked. Write them on the sheet. If a card can't get an owner, it goes back — it isn't this month's move.
0:52Two commitments each. Every person writes one move they will run this week and one brief they will save. Read them in thirty seconds each, or not at all.
0:58Set the revisit. A calendar date, six weeks out, same room. The five cards come back then. Corners move; the check stays.

If the room is quiet

Ask: "which pile was biggest?" Then: "what does it mean that we all already do some of this unmanaged?" Let the piles make the argument.

If the room is sceptical

Run card 06 (check from outside the tool) as the worked example. Sceptics make the best checkers; give them that job by name.

The one rule

Nobody leaves without a name on a card. The deck is not the output; the owners are.
