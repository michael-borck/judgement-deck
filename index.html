What's in it

Five questions you already ask. Now with moves attached.

JUDGE · 10

Trust tool

How much should I trust this output? Two questions place it in a corner; the corner sets the check.

BRIEF · 11

RTCF + push-back

How do I ask for what I need? Brief it like a capable new colleague, and make it argue with you.

CHECK · 10

Check loop

How do I know it's right? Frame, explore, verify from outside the tool, and say what you checked.

DELIVER · 11

Opportunity Finder

Where does AI fit? Spot, assign, prioritise — then checkpoints and a 30-day scale, pivot or kill gate.

PRACTISE · 8

Keep the learning

Two-pass habit, decision rules tested blind, and the part no brief can hold.

How to use it

The guide diagnoses. The deck treats.

One page, five checks

2

Each card is one move: why it goes wrong, what to do, and a line to remember. A few minutes, on real work.

3

Pin the desk card

The printed deck

Also a boxed deck for the table.

Used in workshops and consulting rooms

The deck runs as a session: sort it, argue with it, dot-vote the five moves your team needs first, assign owners. It is printed for courses and client work, and the printable edition on this page is the same content.

Card faces stay typographic on purpose: one move per card, nothing to interpret. Art lives on the box, the back and the wall — never between you and the text.
